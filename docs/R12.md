# R12: read a seal without SQL; a stuck witness can't hold the seal

Written after the fact: R12 shipped in 0.1.1 (c8e5811, 4a272e0) before this file existed.

## show and get

Issue #1 asked for a first experience without SQL. The README's walkthrough verified the example seals with `symbia` and then read them with `sqlite3` queries.

- `symbia show <seal> [--trust <hex>]...` verifies the seal as `verify` does, then prints the verify line, every chain row (seq, thread, kind, lane, key; `(withheld)` for a row a thread seal leaves out), each prediction with its verdict (held, broke, mixed, linked or open, summed over its `results_of` links), and the commands run through `exec` (command, exit, host_ms, chars).
- `symbia get <seal> <key or id> [--trust <hex>]...` verifies the seal, then prints one record in full as JSON: by id, or the newest version of a key, with its links both ways.
- A seal that fails verification is not read: exit 1 and the reason, nothing on stdout.
- The reading lives in `src/read.rs`; the binary only parses arguments and prints.
- The README walkthrough uses `show` and `get`, and the SQL moves to an "It's just SQLite" section.

## The witness wait

On October 10 a prediction sealed at once, and the witness append in `~/Documents` blocked on a macOS privacy prompt. The seal held the store, and every tool call timed out until the prompt was answered.

A seal now writes its witness line on a thread of its own and waits at most `WITNESS_WAIT` (5 s). A write still blocked after that finishes in the background once the folder opens, the seal goes ahead, and the delay goes to stderr. A late line is not yet reported by `status`.

## Tests

- `tests/cli_read.rs`: `show` on the full and thread seals (the full text equals the README block), `get` by key and by id with links both ways, both refusing the tampered copies and an unpinned key, argument order and bad arguments.
- `seal::tests::a_blocked_witness_does_not_hold_the_seal`: a FIFO with no reader as the witness file; the seal returns within 1 s of a 200 ms wait, and the line arrives once a reader opens it.
- `mcp::tests::jobs::tail_bytes_trims_the_tails` now waits for the job's full output, after it raced on a loaded CI runner.
